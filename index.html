Gambit rules

A perfect-information duel: no hidden cards, no luck after the deal. Win by scoring more points on a 6×6 board.

1. Draft

36 cards are laid face up. Players take turns picking in snake order (1, 2, 2, 2, 2 …) until each has 18. The first picker gets the best card, but plays second on the board.

2. Place

Take turns placing one card from your hand on any empty square. All 36 squares get filled.

3. Capture

  • When you place a card, every touching enemy card (up, down, left, right) that is weaker flips to your side.
  • Strength: 2 lowest up to Ace highest. Ties go by suit: ♠ beats ♥ beats ♦ beats ♣.
  • Underdog: a 2 can capture an adjacent Ace of any suit (unless the Ace is linked).
  • Link: a card touching a friendly card of the same suit (◆) cannot be captured while that link stands. All captures from one placement resolve simultaneously, using the links that existed when the card was placed.
  • Cards flip only when a card is placed next to them, so being last to move is a big edge.

4. Win

Squares are worth points: edges 1, the inner ring 2 and the four center squares 3 (56 points in total). The player who moves first gets a komi bonus (shown beside the score) to offset the big last-move advantage. Most points wins; a tie is a draw. Hints are not available in Play Others. You can resign at any time, or offer a draw once the board play begins (the computer accepts only if it isn’t ahead).

Clock

You get 20 seconds per draft pick (the best card is picked for you if time runs out). On the board each player has a chess-style clock that gains time after every move. Run out of time and you lose. Choose the time control on the title screen.

Tips

Selecting a card shows how many cards each square would capture, but the best move is rarely the biggest capture. Save strong cards for the end, build suit links early, and watch which squares your opponent can punish.
